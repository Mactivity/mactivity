- New: **Agents.** Track your AI coding agents (Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Aider and more) in their own sidebar tab: each session's project, the app it runs in, its running time, and the processor and memory taken by it and everything it launched. Stop… ends a whole session after you confirm, and leaves the app or terminal it runs in alone.
- New: **Why is my Mac slow?** One click, in the toolbar, the menu bar window, or Quick Search, explains in plain words what's slowing your Mac right now: an app hogging the processor, memory running short, heat, a full or busy disk, a busy graphics processor, Low Power Mode, or macOS's own background work like Spotlight indexing, Time Machine, Photos, iCloud or an update. Each cause has a button to quit the app or see the details.
- New: **Quick Search.** Press ⌃⌥M anywhere to search apps, processes and ports, like ":3000". Return shows the result in Mactivity, and ⌘⌫ quits it after asking. Change it in Settings → General.
- New: **Keep Awake.** The cup at the foot of the menu bar window, or View → Keep Awake, holds off sleep for 1, 2 or 4 hours or indefinitely, and can keep the display lit as well. It lets go on its own if the battery gets low or the Mac heats up.
- New: **`mactivity mcp`.** Coding agents such as Cursor, Codex and Claude Code can now check on the Mac themselves: what's busy, what's slowing it, which dev servers and agent sessions are up. It can only read. Settings → Command Line copies the line that adds it to Claude Code.
- New: **Record Activity.** View → Record Activity… (⌥⌘R) saves every second's figures, for the Mac and each busy app, to a CSV file until you stop it or an hour passes.
- New: **Since unplugged.** While you're on battery, the Battery tab can show which apps have used it since the Mac came off its charger, and at what time that happened.
- New: **Browser breakdown.** Expand Safari, Chrome or another Chromium browser and its tab processes, extensions, GPU process and network service each get a line, with their portion of the total. Electron apps break down into windows and helpers.
- New: **Your connection.** The Network tab shows the interface, your local IP address, the router, and Wi-Fi signal, speed and channel.
- New alerts: free space on the startup disk below an amount you choose (10 GB to start), your Mac running hot for 5 of the last 10 minutes (with the app working hardest), and, if you turn it on in Settings → Alerts, a lost connection.
- New: **Apps open but unused.** Checkup lists apps you haven't used for days that still hold memory, with a Quit… button for each.
- New: **Hotspot data watch.** On your iPhone's hotspot, the Network tab shows how much data each app has used since you connected, and Mactivity alerts you when an app uses a lot in a short time. You can change the amount, or turn it off, in Settings → Alerts.
- New: **Ask Mactivity.** Type a question into Quick Search, such as "why is my fan loud?" or "which app drained my battery yesterday?", and Apple Intelligence answers on your Mac using its figures and history. Your question never leaves the Mac. Available where Apple Intelligence is turned on.
- Settings → General shows which version you have, beside Check for Updates….
- The Battery tab's list covers the last 24 hours or 7 days, rolling. Plugged in, it shows how the energy divided up between apps instead of an empty list; with time on battery, how the battery did.
- Disk totals are right from the moment Mactivity opens, not only after five minutes.
- Checkup and crash alerts ignore crashes of an app's older versions once you've updated it.
- Storage → Messages Attachments… frees space taken by old Messages photos and videos on this Mac. Pick by age, kind and size, and they go to the Trash. The messages, and everything on your iPhone, stay.
- Storage → Clean Up → iPhone and iPad Backups… shows Finder's backups of your devices, and downloaded iPhone and iPad updates, with their sizes, and moves the ones you tick to the Trash.
- Checkup mentions Messages attachments older than a year and device backups older than six months, when they take real space.
- Storage → Clean Up → Installers and Archives in Downloads… finds disk images, installers and archives left after installing, and downloads that never finished, and moves the ones you tick to the Trash.
- Storage shows the space you can get back, cleanup by cleanup, and what's still in the Trash, with a Review button for each.
- Storage → Clean Up → Mail Downloads… finds copies of attachments Mail saved when you opened them.
- Settings → Privacy shows whether Mactivity has Full Disk Access, what it's for, and how to turn it on.
- Checkup mentions a Trash holding over 5 GB.
- Storage → Clean Up → Developer Caches… frees Xcode DerivedData and package-manager caches that rebuild themselves.
- Checkup tells you when a macOS update is waiting to install.
- Onboarding mentions Full Disk Access, for Storage's cleanups, when it's off.
- The menu bar icon no longer flickers when memory pressure hovers at the warning line.
- Storage → Clean Up → Screenshots and Screen Recordings… finds old captures on your Desktop and in your screenshot folder, in any language, and moves the ones you tick to the Trash.
- Storage → Clean Up → Purgeable Space… explains purgeable space, removes downloads of iCloud files you pick (they stay in iCloud), and lists local Time Machine snapshots. Click Purgeable in the disk's legend to open it.
- When your startup disk is close to capacity, Checkup says how much Storage → Clean Up can recover.
- The `mactivity` command shows network use per app, and `mactivity top --network` sorts by it. Shortcuts' Top Apps has it too when macOS has just opened Mactivity for it.
- The welcome screen offers to open Mactivity at login (ticked), so its history has no gaps.
- If Mactivity opened long after your Mac started and isn't set to open at login, Checkup says history has a gap and points to the setting.
- Growing-memory alerts measure from the app's typical memory at the start of the hour, so one bad reading after the Mac wakes can't make a steady app look like it grew.
- "Your Mac stayed awake last night" no longer fires after a night asleep with Power Nap's short wakes, and Checkup no longer says so either.
- Startup tags an empty plist "Empty", not "Broken": some updaters (Google's) empty theirs to switch off. It no longer counts as starting with your Mac, or as broken in Checkup.
- Time on battery no longer counts time asleep: a day in a bag with Power Nap read as 24 hours. Past days are corrected when you update.
- A Quit… confirmation now shows only where you asked for it, not also in the app list behind a sheet.
- A process that starts with the ID of one that just ended no longer reads as 0 MB for its first second.
- Hotspot data keeps counting across a sleep or a dropped connection, instead of starting again from zero.
- Apps open but unused never suggests quitting Mail, Messages, Slack, Music and other apps people keep open for notifications or playback, or an app that's busy right now.
- Growing-memory alerts come back once an app has released its memory, instead of staying silent until it passes its old peak.
- High CPU, heavy disk and heavy network alerts need the whole window of readings: a busy minute before sleep and a busy second after waking no longer count as 10 busy minutes.
- Quiet hours follow your Mac to a new time zone.
- The daily summary goes out even if your screen stays on all morning, and the weekly summary goes out later in the week if your Mac was off on Monday.
- The uninstaller only ever moves the app you picked and its own files in your Library, whatever its Info.plist says.
- Space you can get back updates right after a cleanup.
- Messages and Mail cleanups judge age by when the file arrived, not when it was last edited.
- Screenshots cleanup keeps working after you change where screenshots are saved.
- You can move files to the Trash from a Startup Disk map in Storage.
- Mail Downloads lists a Pages or RTFD attachment as one item.
- History keeps working after the clock is set ahead and back, and keeps the last minutes before a long shutdown.
- The year calendar's daily figures weigh each hour by how much of it was recorded.
- History's days follow a time-zone change without relaunching.
- Your week's average sleep no longer counts an afternoon nap as a night.
- Compare with the previous period says when there isn't enough history, instead of comparing against an hour of it.
- Right after your Mac wakes, apps' CPU shows correctly instead of all of it landing under macOS for a moment.
- The `mactivity` command and Shortcuts show GPU use per app.
- A download by a short-lived command (curl, brew) now counts toward its app's network use.
- Background services caught at the moment they start no longer show as "xpcproxy" under macOS for good.
- Quick Search's selection stays on the app you picked while the list updates, and macOS's own processes can't be quit from it.
- Checkup's idle dev servers and Quick Search's ports are always current.
- Apps open but unused only lists apps Mactivity has actually seen go unused, and updates after you quit one.
- Why is my Mac slow? says when it has nothing to go on yet, instead of "All clear".
- A Keychain that can't be read for a moment (a declined password prompt) no longer loses your license, and entering your key again never uses another seat.
- A proxy or captive portal's error page during the weekly license check no longer deactivates Mactivity.
- Activation says when you're not really online, instead of showing "HTTP 200".
- Mactivity unlocks as soon as it's back online after a month offline, not hours later.
- Stopping a dev server never stops the editor, REPL or terminal that started it.
- Homebrew services (Postgres, Redis) are no longer shown as a dev project or stopped by Stop Idle.
- Docker containers show when OrbStack or Colima is running, even if Docker Desktop left an old socket behind.
- Stop All stops every server at once instead of one after another.
- Widgets show a big change within 15 minutes, as intended, instead of up to 30.

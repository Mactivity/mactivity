Understand your Mac

The core of Mactivity: what's using your Mac right now, and why.

  • AvailableApps, not process lists

    Helpers, renderers and background services add up under the app that launched them, for processor, memory, energy, disk, network and graphics.

  • AvailableOne-click diagnosis

    Why is my Mac slow? names the cause in plain words, from a runaway app or tight memory to Spotlight indexing, with a button to act on each.

  • AvailableCheckup

    A short list of what deserves your attention: a disk close to capacity, crashing apps, broken startup items, a gap in your history.

  • AvailableNotice slow drift

    Checkup points out an app whose memory has crept up over the weeks, and a battery that empties faster than it did.

  • AvailableBrowser breakdown

    Expand Safari, Chrome or any Chromium browser and its tab processes, extensions, GPU process and network service each get their own line. Electron apps split into windows and helpers.

  • AvailableApps you forgot

    Apps sitting open for days, still holding memory, each with what quitting would free.

  • AvailableYour next Mac

    From a week or a month of use, the chip class, memory and storage to look for in a new Mac, and which Mac to get, laptop or desktop, counting the displays on your desk and how often the lid stays closed, as a one-page PDF.

  • AvailableDisplays and devices

    Every display with its resolution, scaling, refresh rate and connection, each port and what's on it, the USB and Thunderbolt devices plugged in, and whether the lid is closed.

  • AvailableReports

    Save the last 7 or 30 days as a two-page PDF: the figures, the busiest hour and the apps behind it, top apps by every measure.

  • AvailableAsk by typing (optional)

    If you switch it on in Settings, Quick Search can answer questions using Apple Intelligence on your Mac, about now, any day this week, or your coding agents. Nothing is sent anywhere.

  • AvailableSlowdowns you missed

    Open the diagnosis after the moment has passed and it still answers: the times in the last week your Mac struggled, how long each lasted, and the apps that were busiest.

  • AvailableA slow login, explained

    After a restart, see how long your Mac took to calm down and which apps kept it busy, with the ones that open at login marked.

  • AvailableOut of the ordinary

    Each app's usual day, learned on your Mac, and a note when today isn't one: on Overview, in Checkup, on each app's history, and as an alert.

  • AvailableYour day

    Any day on the calendar as a timeline: wakes and sleep, busy stretches and slowdowns with their apps, charging, big downloads, alerts, crashes and agent sessions.

  • AvailableRewind

    Click any bar in a history chart to see the apps behind that moment.

  • AvailableEvery app's own history

    Right-click any app for its last 7, 30 or 90 days: processor, memory, energy and network, time in front, crashes, and the hours of the week it works hardest.

  • AvailableAfter an update

    When macOS or an app updates, the days after are compared with the days before, so you know whether the update made it heavier or lighter.

  • AvailableCompare any two days

    Two days or two weeks side by side: processor, memory, battery, and the apps that changed the most between them.

  • AvailableBeachballs, counted

    Apps that stopped responding: how often, and whether it's getting worse, from the reports macOS already keeps.

  • AvailableUnexpected restarts, explained

    When your Mac restarted on its own, the reason macOS recorded, and what was running just before.

  • AvailableApp launch times

    How long each app takes to open, and a note when one starts taking noticeably longer than it used to.

  • AvailablemacOS's own work, named

    Busy system processes in plain words: Spotlight indexing, Photos analyzing your library, Time Machine backing up.

  • AvailableFlagged by macOS

    What macOS itself caught: an app writing tens of gigabytes to disk, going past its processor limit again and again, apps closed because memory ran out.

  • AvailableCompare two Macs

    Export a report on one Mac and open it on another to put them side by side. A file you move yourself, never a cloud account.

  • AvailableYour month

    A look back at each month: time in use against the month before, the busiest days, apps that came and went, your agents' hours and the slowdowns.

  • AvailableYour year

    The year in a few figures: time in use, the busiest day and month, the apps you used most and the ones new this year, your agents' hours. Shareable as an image.

  • AvailableNotes on your history

    Pin a note to any moment, like “installed the new driver”, and see it on Your day and on every History chart.

  • AvailableTime in calls

    Hours in video and voice calls this week and today, from camera and microphone history, by app and by day.

  • AvailableNotifications by app

    Which apps send the most notifications, and at what times of day. Mactivity keeps only the app and the time, never what a notification said.

  • AvailableFocus streaks

    Your longest stretches in one app before switching, each day, and the apps that break them up most.

  • AvailableBusiest hours

    Each day of the week by the hour: when your Mac works hardest, and when it sits idle.

  • AvailableYour week, on your day

    The weekly summary on the day you choose, Sunday evening included, instead of always on Monday.

Built for developers

For Macs that run dev servers, containers and coding agents all day.

  • AvailableDev servers by project

    Every process listening on a port, grouped by the project folder it runs in, with idle detection and Stop, always after asking.

  • AvailableContainers

    Docker, OrbStack, Colima and Rancher containers with their processor and memory use.

  • AvailableCoding agents

    Sessions of Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Aider and others: project, host app, run time, and the cost of everything each one started. Stop a whole session in one step.

  • AvailableAgent history

    Each agent's sessions remembered by project: time, processor and peak memory for the week, day by day, and each session on Your day.

  • AvailableTalk to your agents

    mactivity mcp lets your coding agent check the Mac for itself, read-only.

  • AvailableCapture a moment

    Record every second's figures to a CSV while a build runs or something feels off.

  • AvailableA command-line tool

    mactivity top, system, projects, agents, day and unusual, with JSON output for scripts.

  • AvailableCatch runaway processes

    An alert when a node, python or similar process sits at a full core or keeps swelling in memory, naming its project and the agent that started it.

  • AvailableBuild times

    Every Xcode, Swift, Rust, Go and JavaScript build timed per project, with its processor and memory, so you can see when builds started getting slower.

  • AvailableSpace per project

    node_modules, DerivedData, target, Python environments and other build folders sized for each project, with the ones you haven't touched in two months moved to the Trash together, after you confirm.

  • AvailableDocker disk use

    Images, volumes, build cache and stopped containers, what nothing uses, and the command that reclaims each, for you to run.

  • AvailableHomebrew services

    What brew services started, its ports, memory and processor, when it was last busy, and the command that stops it.

  • AvailableSimulators and emulators

    Every iOS Simulator device and Android emulator, what the running ones hold, the ones left running after you closed Simulator, and the space each takes.

  • AvailableAgent limits

    An alert when a coding agent's processes hold more memory than you set, or a session runs longer than you set.

  • AvailableDev tools left running

    Language servers, file watchers and test runners by project, including VS Code's and Cursor's, and the ones still running after their editor or terminal quit.

  • AvailableLocal databases, sized

    PostgreSQL, MySQL, Redis and MongoDB running on your Mac: their memory, their data on disk, and the ones showing no sign of use in weeks.

  • AvailableTest run times

    How long swift test, npm test, pytest and cargo test take per project, like build times, with failed and cancelled runs marked.

  • AvailableProjects at a glance

    Each project folder with its language, the runtime version it asks for, its size and when you last worked in it.

  • ExploringLinux machines

    Per-machine figures for OrbStack's Linux machines, alongside their containers.

  • AvailableRemote sessions

    SSH sessions and tunnels open from your Mac, how long each has been open, and the ports they forward, including forwards set in your SSH config.

Battery, heat and sleep

Power figures that explain themselves.

  • AvailableBattery use by app

    Each app's portion of the battery and its watts over any range, from 12 hours to a year, and since you last took the Mac off its charger. Plus what the whole Mac drew: average and peak.

  • AvailableStay awake on demand

    Hold off sleep for an hour or more, or until you switch it off. It lets go on its own when the battery gets low or the Mac heats up.

  • AvailableSleep, explained

    What's stopping the Mac from sleeping, and whether it slept last night.

  • AvailableHeat and fans

    Sensor readings, how fast each fan spins and the thermal state, with an alert if the Mac stays hot.

  • AvailableAccessory batteries

    AirPods, keyboards, mice and trackpads, with a warning before one runs flat.

  • AvailableBattery health over time

    Capacity and cycle count month by month, and roughly when the battery is likely to reach 80% of its original capacity.

  • AvailableWhat woke your Mac

    Each sleep with what ended it: the lid, a key, the network, Bluetooth or a USB device.

  • AvailableBattery lost while asleep

    How much charge your Mac used with the lid closed, and what woke it to use it.

  • AvailableCharger check

    Plugged in, whether your charger covers what your Mac is drawing, and when macOS is holding the battery short of full.

  • AvailableHeat by app

    The apps that take more of the processor when your Mac is warm than they usually do, from the last four weeks.

  • AvailableCharging habits

    How long the battery sits at full charge or charges while hot, in plain terms. Advice only: Mactivity won't set charging limits.

  • AvailableBattery temperature over time

    How warm the battery runs while charging and in use, one of the things that wears it fastest.

  • AvailableChargers compared

    The chargers you use, told apart by their power rating: how many watts each really delivers and how fast it fills the battery.

Disk and storage

See what fills the disk, and get space back safely.

  • AvailableA map of your disk

    Every folder sized and sorted, colored by kind: system, apps, developer files, your own documents.

  • AvailableCleanups that go to the Trash

    Old Messages attachments, device backups, leftover installers, developer caches and more. Nothing is erased; everything you pick goes to the Trash.

  • AvailableActivity per drive

    Reads and writes for each drive, and free space on each volume, with an alert before the startup disk runs short.

  • AvailableSSD health

    Each drive's own report: wear, spare capacity, data written over its life, and a warning when it reports trouble.

  • AvailableWhat grew

    The folders that grew the most since your last scan, so a disk that keeps filling has a name.

  • AvailableTime Machine, watched

    When Time Machine last backed up, and a warning when automatic backups quietly stop.

  • AvailableBig files you forgot

    The largest files you haven't opened in a year, wherever they are, with a Trash button for each.

  • AvailableDuplicate files

    Copies of the same file across your folders, with the extra copies offered for the Trash.

  • AvailableCloud folders, sized

    How much iCloud Drive, Dropbox, Google Drive and OneDrive keep downloaded on this Mac, and which files you could leave in the cloud.

  • AvailableDownloads you never opened

    Files that arrived in Downloads and were never opened afterwards, sized, with the Trash after you confirm.

  • AvailableWhat wears your SSD

    Each app's writes over 30 days beside the drive's own lifetime figure. macOS counts an app's writes across every drive together, so they can't be split by drive.

  • AvailableOld app versions and leftovers

    Second copies of apps and support files of apps that are gone, each explained, nothing ticked, and only ever to the Trash.

  • AvailablePhotos library, sized

    What your Photos library takes on this Mac: originals, edits, thumbnails, shared albums and its database. What iCloud keeps only online isn't counted.

  • AvailableExternal drives, watched

    Space and health of the drives you plug in, remembered with when each was last seen.

Network

Connections you can see, without anything leaving your Mac.

  • AvailableWi-Fi over time

    Signal through the day or the week, and how long it was weak, recorded each minute you're on Wi-Fi.

  • AvailableNetwork changes, logged

    Every switch between Wi-Fi, Ethernet, a hotspot or a VPN, and each drop, with the data used and the apps that used it most. Drops and VPN time show on Your day.

  • AvailableA monthly data budget

    Set your plan's allowance and reset day to see what's left, the pace you're on, each app's share, and an alert before you go over.

  • AvailableWhere apps connect

    The servers each app is talking to right now, in its Get Info window, with their names looked up only if you switch that on in Settings.

  • AvailableNetwork interfaces

    What ifconfig shows, in plain words: every connection on your Mac, what it's for, its addresses, router, DNS, speed and traffic, with a hover on each that says what it means.

  • AvailableCrowded Wi-Fi channels

    Scan to see whether nearby networks share your channel, and which channel looks quieter.

  • AvailableNetwork drives

    Shared folders and NAS volumes you mount: their space, and “Not responding” for a dead one. How busy a share is shows for NFS only.

Security and privacy

What's running, who made it, and what it can reach. Read-only, like the rest.

  • AvailableNew startup items, flagged

    An alert when something adds itself to start with your Mac, naming the app, even when it puts itself back after you turned it off.

  • AvailableOpen to the network

    The ports other devices on your network can reach, by app, what macOS's own are, and whether the firewall is on.

  • AvailableWho made what's running

    Each running app's developer from its code signature, with the ones nobody's name is on set apart.

  • AvailableOutdated software

    Homebrew packages, language versions from pyenv, nvm and friends, Apple's own Python, Homebrew itself and apps with an old built-in updater: what no longer gets security fixes or has known vulnerabilities, with Upgrade for Homebrew packages after you confirm.

  • AvailableCamera and microphone history

    Which apps turned on the camera or a microphone, recorded the screen or read your location in the last week, when, and for how long. Kept on your Mac.

  • AvailableWhat apps are allowed to do

    With Full Disk Access on, which apps can record your screen, read every file, control your Mac or read your keyboard, with the place in System Settings to change it. On macOS 26, the camera, microphone, automation and folders too. macOS 27 keeps those four from other apps, so Mactivity says so and links to each list.

  • AvailableYour security settings

    FileVault, the firewall, Gatekeeper, System Integrity Protection and automatic updates, each on or off at a glance, and in Checkup when an important one is off.

  • AvailableSystem extensions, listed

    Drivers, network filters and security tools that run with the system's own privileges: what each is, and whether it's switched on.

  • AvailableBrowser extensions

    The extensions installed in Safari, Chrome, Edge, Brave, Arc and Firefox, and what each can reach, in plain words. Chrome and Edge need Full Disk Access.

  • AvailableSafe to give Full Disk Access

    Full Disk Access is optional and stays with Mactivity alone: Homebrew no longer inherits it when Mactivity runs it, and every release is checked so no other program can load code into the app.

  • AvailableUnlocks and failed attempts

    When your Mac was unlocked, with Touch ID, a password or your Apple Watch, and failed attempts as macOS logged them.

  • AvailableConfiguration profiles

    The profiles installed for your user, who put them there and the kinds of setting they change, and whether your Mac is managed.

How we decide

  • Your data stays on your Mac. No analytics, no cloud features, no accounts. The only time Mactivity contacts a server on its own is the update check; host-name lookups and the vulnerability check for Homebrew packages happen only if you turn them on.
  • Free, with no subscriptions and nothing billed by use. We won't add features that would need a monthly fee to run.
  • Nothing is erased behind your back. Files go to the Trash, and apps, servers and software upgrades wait until you confirm.
  • Watch the hardware, don't drive it. Mactivity reads fans, temperatures and batteries; it never sets fan speeds or charging limits.
  • Light enough to forget. A monitor shouldn't be the thing slowing your Mac down.
